Honey truth

Is your honey real?

Pure honey is unheated, unblended nectar straight from the hive. It keeps its enzymes, pollen, minerals and antioxidants. Heat, ultra-filtration and sugar syrups take them away. Here's how to spot the difference.

NIHT approvedProduct of South Africa
Five signs · try them

Real honey behaves differently.

The water check

Raw honey drops in a dense strand, sinks and sits at the bottom before it slowly dissolves.

Crystallisation

Raw honeySyrup blend

Raw honey crystallises naturally over weeks or months. That's a good sign: warm it gently to re-liquefy.

Under the loupe

Raw honey carries microscopic pollen, a fingerprint of the flowers it came from.

Season to season

SummerBluegumAutumnMacadamiaWinterAloeSpringWildflower

Real honey varies with the season and the flowers. Uniformity suggests processing.

Sign 5 · traceability

Can they name the flowers and the region?

Authentic producers name the floral source and where it grew. Mass blends rarely can: watch for “blend of EU and non-EU honey”.

On the shelf

Look for these

Crystallises over time · rich floral aroma · “Product of South Africa” · visible pollen in raw honey · NIHT certification.

The honest bit

Home checks are clues, not proof.

Only a lab can prove purity. That's why every Al Khair jar carries a holographic serial you can verify.

Nectar tells the truth

On a white label, the name
is the flower.

Bluegum, Orange Blossom, Litchi: these are the nectar sources the bees visited, not added flavours. Colour, taste and aroma change with the season.

Orange Blossom

Light coloured, with a delicate citrus finish (their own description).

Litchi

From Lowveld litchi orchards in Limpopo and Mpumalanga.

Wild Blossom

Whatever was flowering: every batch differs.

Macadamia

From the macadamia flow, which peaks January to April.

Bluegum

Eucalyptus honey from the summer flow.

Aloe

Darker, robust honey from winter aloe flows.

Nine provinces, one spectrum of nectar

Why South African
honey leads.

  • Fynbos to bushveld

    Cape fynbos, Limpopo and Mpumalanga orchards, Free State sunflowers, wild flora around Kruger.

  • Cleaner sources

    Lower pesticide reliance and stricter controls favour bee health.

  • Naturally low moisture

    The climate gives honey that keeps without additives.

  • Raw and traceable

    Enzyme-rich multifloral honey traceable to living ecosystems, not factory blends.
